Functions: the language of change · Chapter review
Find the domain of $f(x)=\frac{\sqrt{9-x^2}}{x-1}$.
The square root requires $|x| \le 3$; the denominator excludes 1. Intersect these restrictions.
The square root gives a closed interval; remove the denominator's zero.
Let $f(x)=\ln x$ and $g(x)=x^2$. Compare the domains of their two compositions.
The first expression requires only $x^{2} > 0$.
The second evaluates $\ln x$ first, requiring $x > 0$.
Check the inner and outer restrictions separately for each composition.
Why do calculus formulas for trigonometric derivatives use radians?
Radians relate angular change directly to unit-circle arc length, giving $\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin h}{h}=1$.
Degree inputs require conversion by $\frac{\pi }{180}$, adding that factor to the derivative.
Compare $\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin h}{h}=1$ for radian and degree inputs.
Limits and continuity · Chapter review
Find $\lim_{x\to4}\frac{\sqrt{x}-2}{x-4}$.
Multiply by the conjugate to obtain $\frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2}$, then substitute.
Multiply by the conjugate and state where the simplification is valid.
Prove that $x^{2} \to 0$ as $x \to 0$, and give a valid $\delta$.
If $0 < |x| < \sqrt{\varepsilon }$, then $|x^{2}| < \varepsilon$.
Work backward from the output error to an input bound.
A piecewise function equals $2 x + 1$ for $x < 0$ and $x^{2} + k$ for $x \ge 0$. Which $k$ makes it continuous?
The left limit is 1; the right limit and point value are $k$. Set them equal.
Compare both one-sided limits and the value at the join.
Defining and calculating derivatives · Chapter review
Differentiate $y=x^2\sin(x^3)$.
Use the product rule, then multiply the sine derivative by the inner derivative $3 x^{2}$.
The outer structure is a product; the sine term also needs the chain rule.
Find the tangent to $y=\frac{x+1}{x^2+1}$ at $x = 0$.
The function value is 1; the quotient rule gives derivative 1 at 0.
A tangent needs both a point and a slope.
For $x^2+xy+y^2=3$, find $y'$ and the slope at $(1,1)$.
$y\prime=-\frac{2x+y}{x+2y}$; the slope at the point is −1.
Differentiate: $2 x + y + x y' + 2 y y' = 0$.
Collect $y'$ terms and substitute the point.
Use the product rule for $xy$ and chain rule for $y^2$.
More derivatives and models of change · Chapter review
Differentiate $\ln(\sin x)$ and state the domain condition.
The chain rule gives $\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$; the logarithm requires a positive input.
Check the logarithm's domain before differentiating.
A sphere's radius grows at $0.5\,\mathrm{cm}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$. How fast does its volume grow when the radius is 4 cm?
Differentiate the volume relation with respect to time before inserting the radius.
Use the linearization of $e^{x}$ at 0 to estimate $e^{0.03}$. Is it high or low?
Approximately 1.03, an underestimate.
$L = 1 + x$. Since the second derivative of $e^{x}$ is positive, the tangent lies below the curve.
Write the tangent at 0 and use concavity to determine the error direction.
What derivatives reveal about a function · Chapter review
Find the absolute maximum and minimum of $x^3-3x$ on $[-2,2]$.
Maximum 2; minimum −2.
Check critical points $\pm 1$ and endpoints $\pm 2$. The four values are −2, 2, −2, and 2.
Evaluate both interior critical points and both endpoints.
Use the mean value theorem to prove $|\sin b - \sin a| \le |b - a|$.
The inequality holds.
For $a \ne b$, the difference is $\cos c \cdot (b - a)$ with $|\cos c| \le 1$. For $a = b$ it is immediate.
Apply the mean value theorem between the endpoints and bound the absolute cosine.
A length-60 fence encloses three sides of a rectangle against a wall. Find the maximum area.
$2 x + y = 60$ and $A = x (60 - 2 x)$. The derivative changes from positive to negative at $x = 15$.
Then $y = 30$ and the area is 450.
Use the three-side constraint to express area in one variable.
Integrals: accumulation and antiderivatives · Chapter review
Evaluate $\int_0^1 3x^2 e^{x^3}dx$.
Set $u = x^{3}$ and $\mathrm{d}u = 3 x^{2} \mathrm{d}x$. The bounds remain 0 and 1.
Choose the inner function $x^3$ and check both the differential and bounds.
Find $\frac d{dx}\int_{x^2}^{x^3}\sqrt{1+t^2}dt$.
Substitute $x^{3}$ and multiply by $3 x^{2}$ for the upper contribution. Subtract the lower contribution with $x^{2}$ and factor $2 x$.
Subtract the lower-bound contribution from the upper; both need their inner derivatives.
Explain why $\int_{-1}^1x^3dx=0$ does not mean zero geometric area.
The two signed contributions cancel; the geometric area is $\frac{1}{2}$.
Symmetry makes the odd function's integral zero. The absolute-value integral is $2 \int _{0}^{1} x^{3} \mathrm{d}x = \frac{1}{2}$.
A zero integral can result from cancellation. Area requires absolute values.
A continuous function has integral 10 on $[0,4]$. Why must it equal $\frac52$ somewhere?
Its average is $\frac52$; the integral mean value theorem guarantees this value is attained.
Continuity places the average between the minimum and maximum; the intermediate value theorem gives the required point.
The integral supplies the number; continuity guarantees attainment.
Geometric and physical applications · Chapter review
Rotate the region below $y = x^{2}$ on $0 \le x \le 1$ about the $x$-axis and about the $y$-axis. Find both volumes.
About the $x$-axis: $\frac{\pi}{5}$. About the $y$-axis: $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
Disks give $\pi  \int  x^{4} \mathrm{d}x = \frac{\pi }{5}$ over the stated interval.
Shells give $2 \pi  \int  x^{3} \mathrm{d}x = \frac{\pi }{2}$ over the stated interval.
Use disks for one axis and shells for the other; the radii differ.
For $v (t) = 2 t - 2$ on $0 \le t \le 3$, find displacement and distance.
Displacement 3; distance 5.
Displacement is $[t^{2} - 2 t]_{0}^{3} = 3$.
Split at 1: the negative part has magnitude 1 and the positive part contributes 4.
Locate the sign change before accumulating signed displacement and total distance.
A length-2 rod has density $\lambda  = x + 2$. Find its mass and center of mass.
Mass 6; center of mass $\frac{10}{9}$.
$M = \int _{0}^{2} (x^{2} + 2 x) \mathrm{d}x = \frac{20}{3}$. Divide by 6.
Integrate mass and first moment separately, then divide.
A solid has square cross sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis with side $x(1-x)$ for $0\le x\le1$. Find its volume.
The cross section is a square, not a disk.

Domain
The set of allowed inputs.
Composition
The output of one function becomes the input to another.
Inverse function
A function that reverses a one-to-one input–output relation.
Radian
Arc length divided by radius.
Limit
The value approached by outputs as inputs approach a target.
Indeterminate form
A form such as $\frac{0}{0}$ that does not by itself determine a limit.
Continuity
The function value exists and equals the nearby limit.
Asymptote
A line describing a graph's limiting behavior near a boundary or far away.
Difference quotient
The average rate of change between two inputs.
Derivative
The finite limit of a difference quotient as the input separation tends to zero.
Chain rule
Multiply the rates of change through the layers of a composition.
Critical point
A point in the domain where the derivative is zero or undefined.
Inflection point
A point on a continuous curve where concavity changes.
Linearization
A local first-order approximation built from a tangent.
Riemann sum
A sum of sample heights times subinterval widths.
Antiderivative
A function whose derivative is the given function.
Definite integral
The signed limit of increasingly fine accumulated contributions.
Substitution
A change of integration variable based on the chain rule in reverse.
Arc length
The accumulated Pythagorean lengths of short curve segments.
Center of mass
The mass-weighted average position.
Integral mean value theorem
A continuous function on a closed interval of positive length attains its average value there.
Volume by cross sections
Accumulate cross-sectional area along a slicing direction; rotation is not required.
Gauge pressure
Pressure relative to surrounding atmospheric pressure; in a stationary fluid it increases with depth.
Proof supplement: basic trigonometric limits
On the unit circle, for $0 < h < \frac{\pi }{2}$, the inscribed triangle, sector, and circumscribed triangle have increasing areas, giving $\sin h < h < \tan h$. Divide by $\sin h$ and rearrange to obtain $\cos h < \frac{\sin h}{h} < 1$. Since $\cos h \to 1$, squeezing gives right limit 1. The identity $\frac{\sin (- h)}{- h} = \frac{\sin h}{h}$ gives the same left limit. Rationalizing $\frac{\cos h - 1}{h}$ gives $- [\frac{\sin h}{h}] [\frac{\sin h}{1 + \cos h}]$, whose limit is 0.
Proof supplement: Cauchy's mean value theorem
Suppose $f , g$ are continuous on $[a , b]$ and differentiable inside. Define $H (x) = [f (b) - f (a)] [g (x) - g (a)] - [g (b) - g (a)] [f (x) - f (a)]$. Both endpoint values of $H$ are zero. Rolle's theorem gives $H' (c) = 0$, producing the cross-product identity. If $g'$ is nowhere zero inside, the mean value theorem also gives $g (b) \ne g (a)$, allowing division to equate the ratio of increments with a ratio of derivatives.
Proof supplement: the sum of squares
Start with $(i + 1)^{3} - i^{3} = 3 i^{2} + 3 i + 1$ and sum over $i = 1 , … , n$. The left side telescopes. Use $\sum  i = \frac{n (n + 1)}{2}$ and rearrange to obtain $\sum  i^{2} = \frac{n (n + 1) (2 n + 1)}{6}$. This turns the right Riemann sum for $x^{2}$ into an explicit finite expression.
